AI that runs a floating data centre the way a flight computer helps fly a plane.

A live digital copy of the data centre lets our AI test every decision safely before it touches real equipment. It keeps servers cool with less electricity, spots failing parts early and reuses waste heat.

01 The problem

Singapore needs more computing power, but has no land and no spare clean energy

ENERGY

Cooling plays it too safe

Most plants run at fixed, cautious settings all day. That wastes electricity at night and during quiet hours, when servers need far less cooling.

LAND

Nowhere left to build

Floating data centres free up land, but saltwater, marine growth and boat-only access make them harder to run and maintain.

HEAT

Heat goes to waste

Almost every watt a server uses ends up as heat. Most facilities just dump it, often into the sea.

With so little clean power available, Singapore can't buy its way out. Using less energy is the fastest lever we have.

02 Our idea

Four parts, one brain

AEGIS-AQUA is a software layer for floating data centres like Keppel's at Loyang. At its centre is a digital twin: a live virtual copy of the facility that the AI uses to think ahead.

CORE · DIGITAL TWIN

A live virtual copy

Every few seconds, sensor readings update a virtual model of the whole facility, from racks and pipes to pumps, the heat store and the surrounding seawater.

Like a flight simulator for the data centre: try it on the copy first, then do it for real.

PART A · COOLING

Smart cooling

The AI predicts how hot things will get, tests options on the twin and picks the setting that uses the least power while staying safely below the limit.

Eases off at night. Pre-cools before big AI jobs.

PART B · HEALTH

Fault prediction

Spots trouble before it causes a breakdown, such as a pump losing flow or a blocked filter. Cameras with computer vision watch for corrosion, marine growth, cracks and leaks.

Fix it on schedule, not in an emergency.

PART C · HEAT

Heat reuse

Server heat is captured into a separate loop to preheat water on board. The twin plans heat reuse together with cooling, so less heat goes into the sea.

An absorption chiller is an option we'll test, not a promise.

Run from shore

Operators monitor and adjust everything remotely. Crews go out only for servicing, which saves boat trips, cost and emissions.

Ask it anything

An engineering copilot answers plain-language questions like "why did you lower the pumps?" with sensor evidence. Humans approve every action.

This is a simplified model built to show how the controller behaves. Its numbers are illustrations, not measured results. For the hackathon we will rebuild it as a full digital twin based on published specifications and report whatever we actually find.

What the model includes
  • Server load follows a daily cycle, with a training job from 10:00 to 12:00 that the AI can see coming in the job queue. The 14:00 job is a surprise.
  • Rack temperature rises with load and falls with colder coolant or faster pumps, with about a 15-minute delay.
  • The chiller works more efficiently with warmer coolant and less efficiently with warmer seawater. Pump power rises with the cube of speed.
  • AEGIS looks 30 minutes ahead, keeps a 2 °C safety margin below the 32 °C limit and changes settings gradually.
  • The worn pump is flagged when its measured flow drops 10% below what the twin expects. The broken sensor is stuck at a false 23 °C, and AEGIS catches it because a second sensor disagrees by more than 2 °C.
  • Heat is reused only when coolant returns at 26 °C or warmer. CO₂ uses Singapore's 2024 grid emission factor of 0.402 kg per kWh (EMA). A four-room HDB flat uses 380.7 kWh a month (MSE, 2024).

06 Protecting marine life

Warm water can't just be dumped

Many floating data centres together could heat the nearby sea and harm marine life. AEGIS treats the discharge temperature limit as a rule it can never break, on top of standard engineering fixes.

Deep-water intake

Pipes draw colder water from deeper down, so less seawater has to be pumped to carry away the same heat.

Diffuser nozzles

Warm water is spread out so it mixes quickly with the currents, which keeps the warm patch small.

Reuse before release

Heat is reused on board first. At larger scale it could help desalination or LNG regasification instead of warming the sea.

Our addition: the twin watches the sea too

The digital twin forecasts discharge temperature and the size of the warm-water plume. The AI will never pick a setting that breaks the approved limit, so saving energy never costs the sea. Every hour is logged for regulators.

Environmental Impact AssessmentRequired before deployment, with binding measures such as real-time chlorine tracking. Keppel published its EIA in 2025. URA

What's new

Floating data centres, AI cooling and digital twins each exist already. Nobody has combined them for a floating site in the tropics. We bring together:

  1. A twin that protects as well as predictsEvery AI decision is tested on the copy and checked against aviation-style safety rules first.
  2. Faults spotted two waysSensor data plus cameras that watch for marine damage.
  3. Cooling, heat reuse and the sea planned togetherHeat reuse and seawater discharge limits are planned together with cooling instead of separately.

Hackathon targets

For our simulation, not promised real-world results. We'll report real numbers even if we miss.

  • Keep every simulated rack inside its safe temperature limit
  • Catch at least 90% of faults we deliberately inject
  • Measure cooling energy saved against a fixed-setting plant
  • Show the twin stays within 1 °C of the simulated real plant
  • Prove the system drops to safe mode when a sensor fails

Roadmap

  1. HACKATHON

    Simulate

    Build the twin and controller. Compare them with a fixed-setting plant, inject faults and test safe mode.

  2. CALIBRATE

    Match reality

    Tune the twin on Keppel's published design data. Train computer vision on corrosion and marine-growth images.

  3. PILOT

    Advise

    Run on one module in advice-only mode, with operators approving each recommendation.

  4. SCALE

    Control

    Automatic control within approved limits, heat reuse switched on and rollout to more modules.
